## Item 4's question, answered from the source

The C software path uses 8-pixel spans for world surfaces (`d_init.c` line
139: `D_DrawSpans16` only under `USE_X86_ASM` and `d_subdiv16`), 16 for
Turbulent8 and 32 for the sky. Those last two were the GPU's gap, and are
closed; the world's 8 were closed in step 5.

## Item 3 (done afterwards, v0.7.0: see [2026-11-04-surfcache.md](2026-11-04-surfcache.md))

The text below is what was written before it was done, and why it was not
attempted in this step. Bibliography opened: `r_surf.c` (`R_BuildLightMap` line 133,
`R_DrawSurface` 227, `R_DrawSurfaceBlock8_mip0` 319), `d_surf.c`
(`D_CacheSurface` 282), `d_edge.c` (`D_MipLevelForScale` 45, and the call at
247 and 286: `miplevel = D_MipLevelForScale(surf->nearzi * scale_for_mip *
pface->texinfo->mipadjust)`).

What a faithful port needs, and why it was not attempted in this step:

- The light of a surface is not the per-pixel bilinear sample the engine's
  filler takes: `R_BuildLightMap` sums the styles' maps in 8.8, inverts and
  shifts them (`t = (255 * 256 - sum) >> 2`, not below 64), and
  `R_DrawSurfaceBlock8_mipN` interpolates that grid in integer steps over
  blocks of `16 >> mip` texels, left and right edges first, `lightstep =
  (left - right) >> 4`. The baked surface is then `extents >> mip` texels
  square, read by `D_DrawSpans8` with `s` and `t` relative to texturemins.
- The mip level is chosen per surface from `surf->nearzi`, the nearest depth
  over the surface's spans, which exists only in Quake's edge-based hidden
  surface pass (`r_edge.c`, `d_edge.c`); the engine's world path draws faces
  one by one with a painter's order. A port needs a stand-in for `nearzi`
  (the nearest visible vertex), and every face's four mip textures
  (`MipTex.Pixels(level)`).
- The GPU would then need the baked surfaces, or the same block arithmetic and
  mip choice per fragment, plus the matching world reference (the lit
  perspective filler, #63's 8-pixel sub-spans) re-done on the baked surface.

That is a new reference for the whole world path, not an addition to it, and
it would change the world check that step 5 closed at 0.006 %. It is the
next step.

## Cost

Browser (Chrome for Testing 156, wasm, single thread, Apple GPU), 1280 x 800,
the 16 views, the paths alternating frame by frame inside each run; v0.5.2
(a clean clone of the tag) and this step alternated by run, 1-minute load 4.7
to 7.5 for all of them. Medians (min-max) over 3 to 5 runs.

| per frame, 1280 x 800 | v0.5.2 | step 6 |

| software alias pass alone | 0.570 ms (0.550-0.575) | 0.605 ms (0.600-0.615) |
| GPU alias alone, wall | 0.740 ms (0.710-0.765) | 0.915 ms (0.875-0.925) |

Every pass the step touched is slower than before, and none is faster than
the reference's cost class: the software alias pass by 6 %, the GPU alias
pass by 24 %, the GPU sky by 40 % and the GPU water by 33 % (wall time, with a
floor of about half a millisecond, so the sky figures are a larger share of
real GPU time than they look). The GPU costs are the price of the exactness:
the shaders find the scanline run of the face from its polygon per fragment
(sky, water) or work the edge tables and gradients out per fragment (alias).
They are open defects, to be closed with GPU timestamps (go-gfx/webgpu#16) to
see what is GPU time, and by moving the per-face and per-triangle setup out of
the fragment shader. The software alias pass was 1.37 ms in a first version
of the port (the view weapon's spans cross the frame edge and went through a
per-pixel bounds-checking plot) and is 0.605 ms after clipping spans in one
move; natively it is faster than v0.5.2 (0.22 against 0.27 ms per view without
the weapon). `R_LightPoint` costs 0.4 to 0.5 microseconds per entity.
