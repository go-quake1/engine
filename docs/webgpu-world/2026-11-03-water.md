# Water on the GPU, 2026-11-03

Step 4c of the GPU renderer plan: the turbulent surfaces (textures whose name
starts with `*`: water, slime, lava, teleporters) are drawn on the GPU after
the sky and before the entities (`render/gpuworld/special.go`, `fsTurb`).
Method as in [2026-11-03-sky.md](2026-11-03-sky.md).

## Bibliography (what was opened)

- `reference/common/gl_warp.c`: `turbsin` and `TURBSCALE`, `AddWarpVert`
  (the warp from a 256-entry sine table indexed by `(coordinate * 2 + time *
  TURBSCALE) & 255`), `R_UpdateWarpTextures` (GLQuake renders the warp into a
  texture by subdivided quads), the vertex/fragment program version and its
  `WARPCALC` comment (the per-pixel form: `(s + sin((t * 2 + RDT) * TIMEMULT) *
  8) / 64`). `GL_CreateWarpTableTexture` (a lookup texture of the sine).
- `d_scan.c` (`D_DrawTurbulent8`) was **not** opened; the engine's comments
  cite it and I did not check them. The oracle is the engine's port:
  `render.FillTurbulentPolygon` (u' = u + turbsin[floor((v + 40 t) / 16) & 255],
  v' = v + turbsin[floor((u + 40 t) / 16) & 255], peak 8 texels, through
  colormap row 0). This is not GLQuake's warp (index `y * 2 + time * 40.7`, scale
  1/128 or 1/64): the port is a different formula, and the GPU matches the port.
- Opened earlier for the sky and relevant here: nothing else.

## Design

Same as the sky (the same vertex layout, atlas, projection, the left-edge
coverage rule, linear coordinates), with a fragment shader that adds the warp
from the engine's table, written into the shader text from
`render.TurbSinTable()` (a unit test parses it back and compares it bit for
bit) and looks the warped texel up in colormap row 0. Time is the frame
uniform's.

## Cost at 1280 x 800

Seven browser runs, 1-minute load 6.1 to 7.0 before and after (3 runs
discarded above 8), paths alternating frame by frame on the 16 views; medians
(min-max).

| per frame | time |
|---|---|
| Engine software frame: `worldview.DrawEngine` world + `FillTurbulentPolygon` | 11.875 ms (11.780-12.020); p95 25.2 |
| Engine water fill alone | **8.420 ms** (8.290-8.595) |
| GPU world + water, wall until idle | 1.990 ms (1.875-2.125); p95 2.41 |
| **GPU water alone** (lit faces left out of the pass; the PVS walk still runs) | **2.005 ms** (1.930-2.090) |
| GPU world alone, wall | 0.910 ms (0.875-0.950) |

Water alone: 8.42 ms on the CPU against 2.0 ms of GPU wall time, about 4.2
times. This is the heaviest part of the three: the water pass costs about 1.1
ms more than the world alone, where the sky costs nothing measurable. The
views look at water, so it fills large parts of the frame; the fragment
shader does two dynamic table reads and a dependent texture read per pixel,
and the pass is not tuned (no draw merging: one draw per face, 304 faces on
the map). GPU time itself is not measured (go-gfx/webgpu#16).

## What remains

Water is drawn as the engine does, opaque; no transparency (`r_wateralpha`),
no underwater tint, no per-face distance subdivision; merging the per-face
draws; a probe for where the 0.17 % edge difference sits.

## Update: the reference is now Quake's own (step 5)

Step 4's reference for water, `render.FillTurbulentPolygon`, was not the
original, and I had not opened `d_scan.c`. I have now: `Turbulent8` and
`D_DrawTurbulent8Span` (`d_scan.c`), `D_CalcGradients` and the `SURF_DRAWTURB`
branch of `D_DrawSurface` (`d_edge.c`), `sintable` and `TURB_*` (`r_main.c`,
`r_local.h`, `d_iface.h`). Quake's water differs from the port in four ways:

- `s` and `t` are 16.16 fixed point **relative to the face's texturemins**,
  clamped to `[0, bbextents]` (the port used absolute texture coordinates);
- they are perspective-correct at the start of each 16-pixel sub-span and
  stepped linearly in between (the port was affine over the whole polygon);
- the warp is `sintable`, `8 + 8 sin(2 pi i / 128)` texels in 16.16 with
  `pi = 3.14159`, indexed by the other coordinate's integer part (mod 128) plus
  `(int)(time * 20)`, and the texel is `((t + turb[s >> 16]) >> 16) & 63`
  (the port: a 256-entry table of peak 8 indexed by `(coord + 40 t) / 16`);
- the texel is written **unlit** (the port went through colormap row 0).

`render.FillTurbulentQuake` and `render.TurbSpan` port that, `render.NewSurfaceGradients`
ports `D_CalcGradients`; the engine's game and runner now draw water with them.
What stays the engine's own is which pixels a polygon covers (Quake builds
spans from edges; the engine fills convex polygons by scanline, a span starting
at the first pixel of each scanline run, as `Turbulent8` does).

Pinned tests (`render/turb_quake_test.go`): `sintable` entries evaluated from
the source formula (0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 32, 64, 96, 127, 128, 255) and known warp
offsets for a span of known gradients (columns 12 and 13, row 14 at time 0; 13
and 14 at time 0.05), worked by hand from the table.

The GPU shader (`fsTurb`) is rewritten to the original: it rebuilds the
gradients per fragment from the face plane (a 64-byte face table) and the
view, and uses the same table, written into the shader text and compared
entry for entry by a unit test. It does **not** reproduce the 16-pixel
sub-spans (it is perspective-exact per pixel): a sub-span starts at the first
covered pixel of a scanline run, which a fragment does not know.

Same 16 views, same 3 % tolerance (stated in step 4, not changed):

| | region px | differ | unexplained |
|---|---|---|---|
| step 4 (port reference) | 640,675 | 0.17 % | 0.05 % |
| **Quake reference** | 640,483 | 2.50 % | **0.13 %** |
| Quake reference, per-pixel exact (sub-span 1) | 640,482 | 1.35 % | 0.12 % |
| texture one flat colour | 633,901 | 0.16 % | 0.03 % |
| no animation | 640,483 | 2.52 % | 0.11 % |

The planted negative still fails (99.94 %). The GPU, written from the source
independently of the port's code, agrees with the port to 0.13 %; the
sub-span approximation accounts for about half of the 2.5 % of pixels that
differ by one texel (2.50 % to 1.35 % when the reference is made exact) and
changes almost none of the unexplained ones.

Interleaved, 1280 x 800, 7 rounds, load 3.3 to 3.6 (the machine was quieter
than for step 4, so these figures are **not comparable** with the earlier
table): engine software frame with water 6.17 ms (6.09-6.55); Quake water
fill alone 3.04 ms (2.90-3.08); GPU world + water 1.11 ms (0.93-1.51); GPU
water alone 1.21 ms (0.96-1.45); GPU world alone 0.87 ms. The ported
original is cheaper in software than the port it replaces was under load; I
did not re-measure the old fill on the quiet machine, so I cannot say by how
much.
