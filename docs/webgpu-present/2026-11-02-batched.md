# WebGPU presenter, batched texture writes, 2026-11-02

Follow-up to [2026-11-02.md](2026-11-02.md). That run left two defects: the
presenter made two direct texture-write calls next to its single Recorder
submit, and it allocated 42 to 43 objects per frame against 10 for the
software path. Now (engine v0.2.1, go-gfx/webgpu v0.5.0):

- go-gfx/webgpu v0.4.0 adds `Recorder.WriteTexture` (a batched
  `queue.writeTexture`, wire stream version 3, fuzzed; go-wasmcontainers/gpu
  and go-virtio/gpudev build and test against it). The presenter's frame is
  **one JS call** (`replay`), plus one `put` of the new canvas view.
- go-gfx/webgpu v0.5.0 cuts a frame of the batched path from 24 to 4
  allocations (`TestFrameAllocs`, budget enforced): `BeginRenderPass` no
  longer builds and drops the JS descriptor maps (14 allocations), the
  Recorder owns its pass recorders, `CreateView(nil)` passes no object, and
  `Surface.CurrentView` returns the same Go handles every frame.

## Allocations

| | software (`wasm`, `canvas2d`) | v0.2.0 | v0.2.1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| allocs / frame | 10 | 43 | **13** |
| bytes / frame | 584 | 2568 | **608** |

The 3 allocations and 24 bytes left are in `syscall/js`: the closure of the
`try` wrapper around the view call and the argument slices of the two `Call`s
(`put` of the new view, `replay`).

## Is v0.2.1 slower than v0.2.0 at 1280x800? No.

An earlier version of this page reported 1.605 to 1.945 ms for v0.2.1
against a 1.415 ms figure from the first session, and called it a probable
regression. That comparison was not interleaved, and the two v0.2.1 runs
differed from each other by more than the gap. It is retracted. The settling
measurement builds the tags `v0.2.0` (14c0846) and `v0.2.1` (13f38b6) from
clean clones, with the same harness, Chrome for Testing 156 and Apple GPU
(`apple metal-3`, not fallback), 1280x800, webgpu only, rounds alternating
v0.2.0 and v0.2.1, load 2.2 to 5.9, no round discarded.

| frame work p50 (ms) | v0.2.0 | v0.2.1 |
|---|---|---|
| run 1, 7 rounds each | 1.575 (1.560-2.315) | 1.945 (1.570-2.395) |
| run 2, 15 rounds each | 1.595 (1.565-2.355) | 1.595 (1.575-2.295) |

The per-round p50 is bimodal in **both** builds: a fast mode at 1.56 to 1.62
ms and an occasional slow mode at 2.2 to 2.4 ms, which appears in a round
regardless of the build (run 2: slow rounds 3 of 15 for v0.2.0 and 4 of 15 for
v0.2.1, plus two at 1.87). The medians over 15 rounds are equal (1.595 ms
each) and the fast modes are the same, so v0.2.1 is not slower than v0.2.0.
The 7-round median of run 1 (1.945) fell between the modes because 4 of 7
v0.2.1 rounds happened to be slow. The 1.415 ms of the first session was a
different session on a quieter machine: it cannot be compared with these.
The cause of the slow mode (a GPU or power state of the machine, not of the
code) is not established; it is why a median over fewer than about 15 rounds
of this configuration should not be trusted.

So batching the texture writes did not cost CPU time at 1280x800 and the
extra-copy hypothesis is not supported; no change was made. The gain is the
allocation count (43 to 13) and one JS call per frame.

## Reproduce

As in 2026-11-02.md, with the `present` subcommand; the allocation budget:
`GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm go test -run TestFrameAllocs -exec=<wasmbrowser>` in
go-gfx/webgpu. The tag comparison alternates two `bench.RunInBrowser` targets
(one wasm per tag) through `bench.Interleave` with `Present: webgpu`.
